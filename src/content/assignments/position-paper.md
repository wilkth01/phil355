## How it works

You work one case for a month, talk it through in a private conference, and then write about it in two supervised sittings. The work is prepared. The question you are asked about it is not.

1. **Choose a case.** The case bank opens with eight to ten real cases, a page each. Choose one by the deadline. If your tier includes it, you may [write your own case](/assignments/own-case/) instead.
2. **Work it for three weeks.** Whatever you want to use in the sittings has to end up on one page of handwritten notes.
3. **The conference.** You state your position and defend it, and you leave with an objection card.
4. **Sitting one.** You write your position.
5. **Sitting two.** You answer the objection on your card and make whatever revision it forces.

## What AI may be used for

In preparation, AI is open, as it would be for any essay: for research, and for arguing against your own position. Say what you used.

The conference and both sittings are closed.

## How the conference runs

Ten minutes, one student at a time, private and ungraded. You bring a one-page outline and state a position on your case. Professor Wilk works through the five prompts with you. Before you leave, he writes down the objection you handled worst and hands it to you. That is your objection card, and it is the prompt for sitting two.

The conference asks only that you attend with your outline.

## The sitting rules

Both sittings are written in a Word document that is shared with you when the session starts and locked when it ends.

You bring one page of handwritten notes on your case, plus your Logs and your reading tickets on paper. Nothing else.

## What passing looks like

Only the writing is marked, and it is marked by number, not by name. There are four criteria, each a yes or a no:

1. Does the paper use a course concept correctly?
2. Does it argue from the particulars of the case?
3. Does it state an opposing view in a form its holders would accept?
4. Does sitting two answer the objection, or does it repeat sitting one?

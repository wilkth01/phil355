## How it works

Your lab team builds a working thought experiment: something a stranger can do in five minutes that puts them inside one philosophical problem from the first half of the course. Philosophy usually asks people to imagine the case. You can build it.

The spec:

- **Teams of three or four.** These are the lab teams formed in Lab 4.
- **One concept, one interaction, five minutes.** A custom bot, a game, a staged comparison, or a physical object with a digital part.
- **At most two exhibits in the class may be a chatbot and nothing else.** That is settled at the pitch round.
- **A placard of 150 words or fewer.** It gives the claim, the concept and whose it is, and one line headed "What this exhibit can't show you."
- **A visitor trace.** Each exhibit collects one anonymous thing from every visitor: a vote, a choice, or a sentence.
- **Every member docents alone at least once.** Professor Wilk and invited examiners circulate and push back.

Claim little and show it well. No surveys of harms and no posters. Visitor traces stay anonymous, and nobody types personal information into a bot.

Nothing is built over the break.

## What AI may be used for

Building with AI is the assignment. The Logs in the build weeks are where you report on it: what you did, what the AI did, what your team couldn't have done without it, and what it got wrong.

## What passing looks like

At the Crit your team runs the exhibit in full for outside visitors, and it is marked pass or work-in-progress against three standards:

1. A stranger can use it without help.
2. The placard's claim is true to its source.
3. Each member can defend it alone.

A team marked work-in-progress runs again for Professor Wilk at the reset session after break. An exhibit that still falls short is shown anyway, and the team passes by handing in a corrected version by March 25.

The contract also requires your own solo docent turn.
